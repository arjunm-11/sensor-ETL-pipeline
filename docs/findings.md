# What the raw profile tells us (Phase 1)

Interpretation of [data_profile.md](data_profile.md) (generated by `scripts/profile_raw.py`).
Every number below comes from that report.

| # | Finding | Evidence | Implication for the pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty fields are written as **two consecutive spaces**, not omitted. | Splitting on a single space gives exactly 8 positions on all 2,313,682 lines; splitting on whitespace runs gives 3–8. | Parse with a single-space separator. A whitespace split would silently put voltage into the light column for ~93K rows. |
| 2 | Missing values follow a few fixed patterns. | 2,219,803 complete; 92,975 missing only light; 526 have only date/time/epoch; 373 have mote+epoch+voltage only. | Rows without a mote id cannot be attributed → **reject**. Rows missing only some sensor values are kept with NULLs. |
| 3 | Unknown mote ids. | 55 (2,851 rows), 56 (2,507), 58 (4,502), 57 (3), and 6485/33117/65407 (1 each). `mote_locs.txt` only has 1–54. | 55/56/58 look like real working motes (plausible medians: ~21 °C, ~40 % RH, ~2.85 V) but have no location → keep, flag `unknown_mote`. The 3 huge ids are corrupt → flag as well (decision in Phase 3). |
| 4 | **Sentinel / stuck values** dominate the bad data. | Temperature `122.153` × 370,196; humidity `-3.91901` × 212,232 and `-4` × 63,356. | These are not "outliers", they are a fault signature. Range checks catch them, but the report should name them. |
| 5 | Bad readings are caused by **low battery**. | temp > 50 °C rate: 96.3 % at 2.0–2.2 V, 85.8 % at 2.2–2.3 V, 16.9 % at 2.3–2.4 V, ~0 % above 2.4 V. | Voltage is the root-cause signal for the health mart; a "low voltage" flag explains most range failures. |
| 6 | Physically impossible values. | temp > 100 °C: 383,443 rows; humidity < 0: 295,183; humidity > 100: 3,901; voltage up to 18.56 V; humidity down to −8983. | Range checks with flags, never deletes. |
| 7 | **(moteid, epoch) is not a unique key.** | 113,557 groups share (moteid, epoch); median span between them 12.67 days; only 5 groups are < 60 s apart (identical values = same packet received twice). | Epoch counter restarted mid-experiment. Deduplication must use time as well, not just epoch; naive dedup on (moteid, epoch) would delete ~113K real readings. |
| 8 | Epoch ↔ timestamp mismatch. *(Corrected in Phase 3; see below.)* | Phase 1 model `ts ≈ T0 + 31·epoch` left 471,393 rows > 1 week off. Wall-clock seconds per epoch tick per mote: 30–47 (expected 31). | Treat `reading_ts` as the time of record; flag epoch inconsistency rather than "fixing" either value. |
| 9 | File is not time-ordered. | 227,448 lines are earlier than the line before them. | Incremental loading must be by **line range**, not by timestamp. Anything time-based (gaps, daily marts) must sort explicitly. |
| 10 | Motes go silent and deliver far below the nominal rate. | Mote 5 has 35 rows and stopped 2004-03-03; median delivery rate 50.7 % of the 31 s schedule; 440 gaps > 1 h; median last voltage 2.034 V. | Health mart: last-seen, days silent, gap counts, voltage trend. |
| 11 | Few true duplicates. | 0 exact duplicate lines; 5 same-packet duplicates (differing only in arrival microseconds). | Dedup rule is small in effect but required for idempotency guarantees. |
| 12 | Reference file quirks. | `connectivity.txt` has 1 blank line and 1 line with only 2 fields. | Tolerate both when loading. |

## Phase 3 investigation (on typed staging data)

Queries were run against `staging.sensor_readings`. These results set the thresholds in `sql/transform/clean.sql`.

**Epoch (corrects #8).** The 471K figure was an artefact of my Phase 1 model, not of the data. Epoch is a **network-wide 16-bit counter** advancing ~2,880 ticks/day (≈30 s/tick, not 31 s, hence a steady drift of ~0.8 h/day under the 31 s model). It wraps 65535 → 0 on 2004-03-21, which explains the 12.67-day "duplicates" in #7. Individual motes also reboot and restart near 0. Measured against the *median epoch of all readings in the same hour* (with wrap-aware distance), 2,294,394 readings (99.2 %) are within 1 h (120 ticks), 648 within 1–2 h, 812 within 2 h–1 day and 17,302 more than 1 day off.

**Temperature vs voltage.**

| temperature band | rows | median V | share < 2.4 V | median humidity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 30 °C | 1,835,040 | 2.582 | 10.5 % | 40.6 |
| 30–35 °C | 52,659 | 2.628 | 18.4 % | 27.1 |
| 35–40 °C | 9,027 | 2.320 | 70.4 % | 44.0 |
| 40–50 °C | 7,919 | 2.302 | 99.9 % | 47.0 |
| 50–122 °C | 33,463 | 2.293 | 100 % | 46.1 |
| ≥ 122 °C | 374,134 | 2.184 | 100 % | −3.9 |

30–35 °C readings come from healthy motes with low humidity (warm, dry: plausible). From 35 °C up they come from failing batteries. Temperatures above 50 °C rise smoothly toward the stuck 122.153 value. That's gradual drift, not random spikes.

**Voltage cutoff.** In every 0.05 V bin below 2.30 V the median temperature is 122.15. Of 403,751 readings below 2.30 V, only 23,780 (5.9 %) have both temperature and humidity in plausible ranges. In 2.30–2.35 V it's 69,989 / 105,941 (66 %), and at ≥ 2.35 V it's 1,801,861 / 1,803,464 (99.9 %).

**Voltage range.** 7 readings < 1.8 V (0.009–0.39 V) and one 18.56 V (mote 32) are glitches. Dying motes report down to 1.856 V. Motes 20 and 55–58 legitimately report 3.0–3.19 V.

**Spikes.** Among plausible readings (0–50 °C, ≥ 2.3 V), consecutive readings of the same mote within 120 s differ by a median of 0.01 °C, p99.99 2.48 °C. Only 9 pairs differ by > 5 °C. Contextual outliers are rare once range and battery rules apply.

**Light.** No negatives. 139,079 readings sit at the sensor maximum 1847.36 (saturation in bright light), so that value is real.

**Batches vs time.** Every 500K-line batch spans 34–38 of the 38 days in the data, so neighbour-based rules can't be evaluated per batch.

**End of deployment.** Registered motes report ~1,400 readings/hour until 2004-04-01 21:00, then drop to < 100/hour after 2004-04-02 00:00. Only motes 23–25 and 34 (registered) and 56/58 (unregistered) continue in a trickle until 2004-04-05. "Last hour with ≥ 10 % of registered motes reporting" gives the network end time. Thresholds of 5 %, 10 %, 20 % and 30 % all yield 29 motes silent > 24 h before it.

## Phase 4 findings (from the marts)

- **Sampling period is 30 s, not 31 s.** Regressing the network's hourly median epoch on time (2004-03-01 to 03-20, between wraps) gives 30.001 s/tick, r² = 0.9999999. Expected readings use 2,880/day.
- **Two network-wide outages.** No registered mote reported 2004-03-10 10:00 to 03-11 09:00 (23 h) or 2004-03-15 17:00 to 03-16 11:00 (18 h): 41 of 816 network hours. These are base-station/logging gaps, and they are most motes' "longest gap".
- **Mote 28 never reports light.** All 46,441 of its readings have an empty light field. That's half of all `light_missing` rows; its temperature data is mostly usable.
- **Most deaths are battery deaths, one is not.** 28 of the 29 dead motes had a daily average below 2.30 V before going silent. Mote 50 stopped on 2004-03-11 with a last-day average of 2.558 V, so the battery wasn't the cause.
- **Delivery.** Even alive motes delivered about 50 % of the 30 s schedule (average delivery_rate 0.501 for the 25 alive at the end), so lossy radio links are normal here, not a per-mote fault.
